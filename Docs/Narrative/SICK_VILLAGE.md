# DARKWELL — 病村 (Sick Village)

Status: canon settlement seed. This document records only currently approved facts for the first major settlement. Details marked TBD must not be invented into canon without approval.

## 1. Settlement identity

The first major collected world is called **病村** (working English label: **Sick Village**).

病村 was originally a village that developed beside a mine. The settlement grew because of the mine and depended on the mining economy.

Current locked facts:

- the village exists because of the nearby mine;
- the mine is an important part of the settlement's history, economy, geography, and likely exploration structure;
- the exact mineral/resource being mined is TBD;
- the exact era, technology level, ownership structure, and size of the mine are TBD.

## 2. Author-only hidden prehistory

The following chain is **true canon but hidden from the player until the late 病村 reveal**:

1. miners uncover a clue / trace connected to the unnamed god underground;
2. one or more miners are directly altered / mutated after contact with the discovery;
3. the abnormal miner(s) are brought for treatment;
4. the village doctor encounters the god's existence through examining or treating the mutation;
5. the god begins influencing / tempting the doctor;
6. under that influence, the doctor poisons the village well and creates the epidemic crisis.

These events are not part of the player's early understanding of 病村.

The exact object, structure, remains, symbol, substance, voice, or phenomenon discovered by the miners is still TBD.

The miners' mutation is **not** currently defined as a deliberate god-given blessing. It is an abnormal consequence of contact with the discovered trace. Controlled blessings are reserved for the collected otherworld after the village is taken.

## 3. The public catastrophe: plague

病村's apparent extinction-level disaster is a **plague / epidemic**.

The story presented to the player through most of the 病村 arc is deliberately incomplete:

> A terrible plague suddenly overwhelms the mining village. Ordinary means fail. The villagers turn to an ancient protection rite. The god answers, ends the crisis, and saves the village.

This is the version believed by most inhabitants and the version the player should initially accept or at least consider plausible.

The hidden canon truth is:

> The epidemic was deliberately initiated when the doctor, already under the god's influence, poisoned the village well.

The god therefore helps create the conditions for its own apparent miracle through human action.

Still TBD:

- the plague's exact symptoms;
- transmission beyond the poisoned water source;
- mortality pattern;
- what exactly the god tells or shows the doctor;
- how fully the doctor understands the consequences when poisoning the well;
- whether the doctor rationalizes the act as treatment, experiment, revelation, necessity, sacrifice, or something else.

Do not soften the locked causal fact: the doctor poisons the well under the god's influence, and this act causes the disaster that the god later appears to solve.

## 4. The doctor and the god

The **doctor** is the first major NPC to become aware of the god's existence through medical treatment of the mutated miner(s).

This fact is also part of the late hidden prehistory and should not be given to the player early.

Current locked responsibility:

- Miners: uncover the first trace and suffer the first mutation.
- Doctor: discovers / becomes aware of the god while treating the mutation, is then influenced by it, and ultimately poisons the village well.
- Pastor: later performs or leads the communal protection / summoning ritual after the plague becomes catastrophic.

The doctor's attempt to understand an impossible patient becomes the route through which the god gains a human agent in ordinary reality.

## 5. The pastor performs the summoning

The village's **pastor / cleric (牧师)**, not the doctor, presides over the actual summoning/protection ritual.

From the public point of view, this is the central salvation event of 病村.

The pastor takes the ritual knowledge available during the crisis and turns it into a communal act of salvation.

The exact relationship between this pastor and the currently planned **voiceless high priest** is not yet explicitly locked. Do not assume they are necessarily the same person unless later confirmed.

The pastor's original denomination, prior beliefs, reason for accepting the rite, and exact ritual procedure remain TBD.

## 6. Power boundary: reality versus the collected otherworld

A key rule for 病村 and the wider setting is locked.

### In ordinary reality

The god **cannot freely grant controlled supernatural blessings**.

Its primary influence is temptation, suggestion, corruption, persuasion, dreams, visions, compulsions, or other forms of psychological / spiritual manipulation through exposed human beings.

This is why the god needs the doctor to poison the well rather than simply creating the epidemic through an overt miracle.

The mutation caused by the miners' discovery is abnormal contact with a trace connected to the god, not the same kind of deliberate blessing available later.

### In the collected otherworld

Once the village has been removed from ordinary reality and entered the god's collection, the rules change.

The god can directly:

- take parts of people as prices / offerings;
- alter bodies, minds, memories, emotions, identities, or relationships;
- grant abnormal abilities / "blessings";
- sustain and distort the sacrificial cycle.

This boundary preserves the god's indirect, manipulative quality before collection and its overwhelming authority after collection.

## 7. The salvation ritual is the trap

When the plague reaches the point where the village believes it cannot survive, the pastor leads the protection / summoning ritual.

The ritual appears to succeed:

- the plague crisis is stopped, neutralized, or no longer proceeds in the expected way;
- the village survives the immediate extinction event;
- the participants interpret this as divine rescue.

The hidden reality is that the settlement has been **removed from ordinary reality and added to the god's collected worlds**.

The complete author-level causal chain is therefore:

> god's trace appears -> doctor encounters the god -> god influences the doctor to create the plague -> village suddenly falls into catastrophe -> villagers actively ask the god for salvation -> the god appears to end the disaster -> the village enters the god's domain -> the god gains direct power to price, alter, and bless the inhabitants.

The first three causes in that chain are hidden until the late 病村 reveal.

For most of the arc, the player should only perceive:

> plague -> desperation -> protection ritual -> apparent salvation -> collected world.

## 8. The first communal sacrifices are other people

The core six major NPCs are **not** to be framed as heroic founders who willingly sacrificed themselves in order to save everyone else.

The protection / summoning system uses **other people as communal sacrificial victims**.

This is an important moral direction for 病村:

- the central figures accept or participate in a system that spends other people;
- the village's apparent salvation is built on victims who are not the main cast;
- the core cast should not be retroactively purified by revealing that their mutilations were selfless sacrifices for the community.

The identities, selection rules, consent/coercion, social status, and exact number of the initial victims remain TBD.

## 9. The ritual participants are immediately priced and blessed after collection

A locked rule applies to the NPCs who actively participate in the salvation ritual:

> **Upon entering the collected otherworld, the god immediately takes the personal price associated with their desire and grants the corresponding blessing.**

This happens only after collection, consistent with the rule that the god cannot grant full blessings while the village remains in ordinary reality.

This personal price is distinct from the communal victims used by the ritual itself.

The ritual therefore contains two layers of exploitation:

1. **Other people are offered as the communal sacrifice used to obtain salvation.**
2. **The participating major NPCs are personally altered after entering the otherworld, losing something essential and receiving a maliciously fulfilled ability.**

Their personal bargains remain fundamentally selfish. Their losses should not be reframed as heroic self-sacrifice for the village.

Existing character direction remains:

- the hunter loses the eyes most important to hunting in order to become a superior hunter;
- the doctor loses the arms most important to normal medicine in pursuit of greater medical power;
- the ritual authority loses the voice required for prayer and spoken liturgy while gaining abnormal ritual influence;
- the village head loses the legs required to physically lead while gaining abnormal control over followers;
- the mother loses the memories that made her missing child meaningful to her in exchange for the child's return.

Exactly which of the six major NPCs were direct participants in the original salvation ritual is still TBD unless separately confirmed later.

## 10. Relationship to the first-map faith stage

病村 is the first major map and belongs to the **salvation / fanatic gratitude** stage defined in `WORLD_PROGRESSION.md`.

The dominant public belief is still:

> The god saved us from the plague.

The village therefore provides the player's first misleading model of the system:

- the plague appears to have struck suddenly;
- ordinary responses appear to fail;
- the summoning appears to work;
- the village still exists;
- recurring sacrifice is rationalized as the necessary price of continued protection;
- important believers can point to their abnormal powers as evidence that the god answers requests;
- the true cost and inevitable decay of collected worlds are not yet fully understood by the player.

The late reveal must reinterpret this gratitude completely:

> The god did not merely answer a disaster. Its trace was uncovered first; it reached the doctor before the epidemic; the doctor then created the epidemic under its influence; only after the village had been driven into desperation did the god present itself as the savior.

This should be one of the major revelations that changes how the player interprets both the doctor and the god.

## 11. Reveal-order guardrail

This section is a hard narrative guardrail.

### Early and middle 病村: player-visible story

The player may learn:

- 病村 was a mining village;
- a plague suddenly devastated it;
- normal attempts to survive failed;
- a pastor led a protection ritual;
- sacrifices were involved;
- the god answered;
- the plague stopped;
- the village survived but now exists somewhere abnormal;
- the villagers believe they were saved.

The player may encounter vague inconsistencies, missing records, impossible chronology, suspicious behavior from the doctor, or traces that something happened in the mine, but these clues must not resolve the hidden chain.

### Late 病村: hidden chain revealed

Only near the end of the 病村 story should the player be able to establish the full missing prehistory:

1. miners first uncovered the god's trace;
2. the miners mutated;
3. the doctor encountered the god while treating them;
4. the god influenced the doctor;
5. the doctor poisoned the village well;
6. the resulting plague created the desperation that led the village to summon the same god responsible for engineering the crisis.

The intended reversal is:

> **The god was present before the disaster, not summoned because of it.**

That realization is the key 病村 twist.

Do not expose the miner discovery, the doctor's first contact with the god, or the doctor's role in creating the plague as confirmed facts in early NPC dialogue, tutorial text, ordinary item descriptions, quest summaries, map labels, or other routine exposition.

Foreshadowing is allowed; confirmation is not.

## 12. Story-design workflow

病村 should continue to be designed fragment by fragment rather than filled in for completeness.

Approved workflow:

- record strong ideas as canon when explicitly approved;
- distinguish approved facts from proposals;
- let the Writer / Narrative Designer connect approved fragments into chronology, causality, environmental storytelling, dialogue, and reveal order;
- preserve TBDs instead of inventing connective lore prematurely.
