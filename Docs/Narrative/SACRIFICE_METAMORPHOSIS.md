# DARKWELL Sacrifice, "Pupa," and Metamorphosis

Status: canon supplement to `NARRATIVE_FOUNDATION.md`.

This document records the current narrative direction for how collected worlds describe external sacrificial substitutes, what actually happens when the player is sacrificed, and how the sacrifice system should interact with progression without turning DARKWELL into a conventional roguelite.

## 1. The player is consumable, but not literally burned

The design may describe the player as a scarce sustaining resource at the system level, but the sacrifice is not a literal combustion process.

A completed sacrifice takes **a part of the sacrificed person** and casts/offers that part into the well.

The taken part can eventually include physical, sensory, emotional, cognitive, relational, or identity-bearing parts of a person. Exact sacrifice tables and which parts can be taken from the player remain TBD.

The important rule is that a person can only be used a finite number of times. Each successful sacrifice makes that person less complete. The collected worlds therefore treat a newly arrived, relatively intact marked outsider as an unusually valuable substitute.

Only these marked sacrificial outsiders / substitutes are currently intended to be able to cross the boundaries between collected worlds reliably. Ordinary inhabitants are bound to their collected world and its loop. The exact metaphysical mechanism remains TBD.

## 2. In-world term: "Pupa" / 蛹

Collected-world inhabitants use a deliberately misleading ritual term for an outside sacrificial substitute:

**蛹 (Pupa)**

The word is chosen because it sounds like a state of transformation rather than consumption.

A newly arrived player should not immediately understand that "蛹" means a person whose body, memories, emotions, identity, or other essential parts can be repeatedly taken to sustain a collected world.

In early / younger worlds, the term can carry positive or sacred connotations. A believer may sincerely describe a 蛹 as someone chosen to transform, shed an old self, or approach the god.

Deeper worlds may use the same word much more coldly, exposing that it has become a practical category for a limited sacrificial resource.

The term should help the story conceal the real function of sacrifice during the early game.

## 3. Sacrifice is called "molting" / 蜕 or 蜕皮

The sacrificial process is euphemistically described as **蜕**, **蜕皮**, or an equivalent local ritual phrase.

The official religious explanation is that the 蛹 does not lose something valuable. Instead, it supposedly sheds an unnecessary old part on the way toward a higher state.

This interpretation is intentionally deceptive.

What actually happens is:

1. The player/NPC is successfully delivered for sacrifice.
2. The rite removes a meaningful part of that person.
3. That part is offered into the well and used as part of the world's continued maintenance/delay of collapse.
4. The sacrificed person is restored by the loop, but not completely.
5. The resulting absence is filled, compensated for, or distorted by supernatural change.

The person therefore returns **more capable in some way but less human / less complete in another**.

## 4. The false promise of "metamorphosis"

The first map should encourage both the player character and the player at the keyboard to misunderstand the system.

When the player fails a Monday-Saturday night and is sacrificed, the next morning can include a new persistent benefit, resistance, abnormal capability, or other positive progression effect.

Early presentation should make this resemble a genuine reward:

- the player failed, but became stronger;
- the rite is called molting rather than mutilation;
- believers insist that each molt is progress;
- the new abnormal ability appears useful immediately.

The narrative truth is darker: the "reward" is **distorted compensation for something that has been removed from the player**.

The player should initially be capable of interpreting sacrifice as a beneficial failure-progression system. Later story evidence should recontextualize earlier upgrades as signs that the player has already been consumed in pieces.

This is an intentional player-facing deception, not merely hidden lore.

## 5. Distorted compensation

The god's existing malicious-fulfillment rule also applies to repeated sacrifice.

When a part is taken, the player may receive an abnormal compensation that makes the loss look like improvement.

Illustrative examples only (not locked upgrade content):

- a physical function is reduced or removed, but the player gains an abnormal resistance;
- a sensation is taken, but a related combat penalty disappears;
- an emotion is weakened, but the player becomes resistant to an associated supernatural effect;
- a memory or relationship is removed, but some form of perception or access is granted in return.

Exact player sacrifices, buffs, debuffs, visual changes, dialogue changes, and mechanical consequences remain TBD.

The guiding principle is locked:

> Every "upgrade" obtained through sacrifice must also represent evidence that the player is becoming less complete.

## 6. Finite sacrificial capacity

A 蛹 cannot molt forever.

The number of successful sacrifices a person can endure is finite because there are only so many meaningful parts that can be removed before the remaining person can no longer function as a usable sacrificial substitute.

The exact count is not yet defined and does not need to be a simple visible numeric life counter.

NPCs in deeper worlds may understand sacrificial capacity more clearly than people in the first world. Their language and behavior can reveal that they are evaluating how much of the player remains, without exposing a conventional game-stat vocabulary.

What happens when a person has nothing meaningful left to take is intentionally TBD.

## 7. "Emergence" / 羽化 remains religious doctrine, not confirmed truth

The metamorphosis metaphor can include the idea of **羽化**: believers claim that repeated molting leads to a final higher state.

This is useful as an early religious explanation for why sacrifice should be welcomed rather than feared.

However, the project does **not** currently confirm that any genuine higher state exists.

Possible later interpretations include:

- 羽化 is pure propaganda;
- it is the point at which nothing remains to be sacrificed;
- it is total absorption into the god/collection;
- it is something stranger that no living witness can verify.

Until explicitly decided, no successful example of true 羽化 should be treated as canon.

## 8. Relationship to NPC sacrifice

The same conceptual rule reinforces the ordinary-night pursuit structure.

A designated NPC hunts the player because the player can substitute for that NPC's own required sacrifice.

If the NPC fails, the NPC is sacrificed and returns the next day more incomplete.

If the player is captured, the player undergoes another "molt" instead.

This creates a deliberately cruel exchange:

- surviving preserves the player but further damages a familiar NPC;
- being captured preserves the NPC for that night but consumes another part of the player;
- neither outcome defeats the system that demands the sacrifice.

Exact NPC degradation remains authored per character and is still TBD.

## 9. Genre guardrail: weak roguelite influence, not a conventional roguelite

The sacrifice/molt system creates a risk that DARKWELL could drift into a build-centric roguelite. That is not the intended direction.

DARKWELL remains primarily an authored survival-horror exploration game with nonlinear, interconnected fixed worlds.

The project may borrow one useful roguelite idea:

> Failure can create lasting content and progression instead of only forcing a reload.

But the game should not be built around repeated randomized runs or farming death for a build.

Current guardrails:

- major maps and important spatial connections are authored and persistent, not procedurally rebuilt every run;
- exploration knowledge, story discovery, important shortcuts, and world understanding should remain central forms of progression;
- sacrifice-derived transformations should be few, authored, consequential, and narratively legible rather than a large pool of random perks;
- the player should feel conflicted about being sacrificed, not simply optimize around dying for upgrades;
- a sacrifice-derived benefit must carry narrative evidence of loss or distortion;
- the core fantasy remains exploring and understanding the connected collected worlds, not repeating runs to assemble a build.

Exact persistence rules, Sunday failure consequences, and how much mechanical progression survives larger resets remain TBD and belong to joint narrative/gameplay design.

## 10. Intended reveal arc

The terminology should support the larger world-progression structure.

Early world:

- "蛹" sounds sacred or fortunate;
- "蜕皮" sounds like growth;
- the player can reasonably believe that failure created a useful transformation;
- NPCs praise the god's ability to change and preserve people.

Middle world:

- residents are less reverent about molting;
- damaged people reveal that every transformation also removes something;
- the player begins to understand that these are not free upgrades.

Deeper world:

- the euphemism has largely collapsed;
- people openly evaluate how much remains in a 蛹;
- repeated "metamorphosis" is visibly indistinguishable from progressive destruction;
- the player's earlier rewards are reinterpreted as evidence of prior sacrifices.

The desired realization is:

> I thought the game was rewarding me for surviving failure. It was showing me how much of my character had already been taken.

## 11. Open questions

The following remain intentionally unresolved:

- exact parts the player can lose;
- whether losses are chosen, semi-random, NPC-dependent, world-dependent, or authored in sequence;
- the exact number of sacrifices a player can survive;
- whether sacrifice benefits are always paired with hidden costs or sometimes obvious costs;
- how visibly the player's body changes;
- whether sacrificed memories remove dialogue choices, map knowledge, relationships, or other content;
- the exact meaning, if any, of 羽化;
- what happens when a 蛹 is fully exhausted;
- exact Sunday death/reset behavior beyond the existing rule that Sunday defeat does not use the ordinary next-morning recovery;
- how this system interacts with save/load and long-term campaign persistence.
