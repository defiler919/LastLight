# DARKWELL World Progression

Status: canon supplement to `NARRATIVE_FOUNDATION.md`.

This document records the currently agreed narrative progression across the early collected worlds. It defines the dominant social attitude toward the unnamed god and the way the player's understanding of the god changes with depth. Individual NPCs may deviate from the dominant attitude, but each map should have a clearly readable overall stage.

## 1. Core progression rule

The deeper the player travels, the older the collected world is and the longer it has lived under repeated sacrifice.

Depth therefore reveals two things at the same time:

- the physical corruption of the collected worlds;
- the psychological collapse of the people living inside them.

The player should not begin the game already knowing that the god is deceptive. The god's true nature is uncovered gradually by comparing several worlds at different ages.

The intended progression is not simply `normal -> harder monsters -> ruined map`. It is also:

`gratitude -> doubt -> resentment -> decay -> understanding`.

Each deeper world acts as evidence of what the previous, younger world may eventually become.

## 2. First major map: salvation and fanatic gratitude

The first collected settlement is comparatively young.

Its people still believe that the god saved them.

Dominant attitude:

- gratitude;
- active worship;
- optimism about the god's protection;
- strong willingness to perform sacrifice;
- belief that sacrifice is a necessary and honorable price for continued survival.

The inhabitants remember the catastrophe that should have destroyed them and can point to the apparent miracle: the disaster stopped and the settlement survived.

Most major NPCs therefore do not initially think of themselves as victims of the god. Even when the weekly and daily sacrifices are cruel, they can interpret them as proof that the god continues to protect the settlement.

The first map should allow the player to understand why reasonable people could become believers before the story demonstrates the cost of that belief.

The player should encounter contradiction and unease, but not receive a complete explanation of the deception here.

## 3. Second major map: the cost becomes visible

The second collected settlement is older and has endured substantially more sacrifice and repetition.

Its people no longer share the first settlement's uncomplicated gratitude.

Dominant attitude:

- exhaustion;
- doubt;
- resentment;
- bargaining with faith;
- recognition that the god continually demands more;
- fear of abandoning the sacrificial system even after trust has begun to collapse.

People still participate because the alternative appears worse, but many have begun to ask whether they were truly saved.

The settlement should contain clearer evidence that sacrifice does not solve the underlying problem. It only postpones deterioration.

NPCs can disagree sharply:

- some remain loyal because sacrifice still produces visible short-term benefits;
- some believe the god's demands have become intolerable;
- some want to resist but have seen resistance fail;
- some no longer believe, yet continue the rites out of fear;
- some have stopped caring and are beginning to sink into fatalism or self-indulgence.

By the end of this map, the player should strongly suspect that the god's protection is not salvation.

## 4. Third major map: corruption and rot

The third collected settlement is old enough that the consequences can no longer be rationalized away.

The world has begun to "rot."

Rot is not only visual decay. It affects:

- bodies;
- memory;
- identity;
- architecture;
- relationships;
- ritual behavior;
- the boundary between day and night;
- the distinction between human beings, god-touched followers, and anomalies.

Dominant attitude:

- despair;
- spiritual and social collapse;
- compulsive ritual without genuine faith;
- people continuing traditions whose original purpose they barely remember;
- open hatred of the god mixed with terror of stopping the sacrifices;
- increasingly damaged NPCs whose repeated offerings have stripped away important parts of themselves.

Some inhabitants may still call themselves believers, but belief at this depth should look very different from the first map's gratitude. It can become dependency, terror, habit, fanatic denial, or an attempt to find meaning in irreversible ruin.

The third map is where the player should receive strong evidence that the god did not truly rescue these settlements. It placed them in an extradimensional collection whose destruction was merely delayed.

## 5. Narrative reveal strategy

The god's nature should be revealed through comparison rather than exposition.

The first map says, in effect:

> The god saved us.

The second says:

> The god saved us, but why does salvation cost more every week?

The third says:

> This was never salvation.

The player should gradually infer that the apparently stable first settlement is simply an earlier stage of the same process already visible below.

A powerful recurring realization should be:

> "This is what the people above will eventually become."

This lets world depth function as narrative foreshadowing.

## 6. Later worlds

Later stages beyond the third map are intentionally not locked yet.

They should continue the same principle: deeper worlds have endured the god's collection and sacrificial economy for longer, and therefore expose progressively more severe consequences and older layers of truth.

Possible later stages may include near-total collapse of personal identity, settlements reduced to ritual roles, severe cross-world overlap, or worlds whose inhabitants no longer remember the original disaster or why sacrifice began. These are proposals only until explicitly approved.

The god's exact origin, identity, motive, and whether it deliberately causes every catastrophe remain unresolved. Increasing depth should reveal more evidence without requiring the game to provide a complete cosmological explanation.
